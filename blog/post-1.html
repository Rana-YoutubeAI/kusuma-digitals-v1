How Much Does a
Website Cost?

Your neighbor paid $200. Your competitor's agency charged $15,000. Both numbers can be right — here's why, and what you should actually budget.

By Rana  ·  June 1, 2026  ·  4 min read  ·  Back to Blog

One of the most common questions I get is some version of: "I got a quote for $800 and another one for $8,000 — what's the difference?" The honest answer is that website pricing is genuinely confusing, because the range is legitimate. A $200 site and a $15,000 site can both be real, appropriate prices for different situations.

Here's how to make sense of it.

The Three Tiers of Website Pricing

DIY Builders — $0 to $50/month

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, and GoDaddy Website Builder let you build a site yourself using drag-and-drop templates. The price is low because you're supplying all the labor.

These tools are fine for a business that has one service, a simple location, and not much competitive pressure. The tradeoff: your site looks like a template, because it is one. Every other Squarespace restaurant in St. Louis has a version of the same layout. And the SEO tools are limited — which matters more than most people realize.

Freelancer or Small Studio — $500 to $5,000

This is the widest range because experience and scope vary enormously. A college student building a site on the side might charge $400. An experienced freelancer with a track record might charge $2,500 to $4,000. Both are freelancers. The difference is in what you get: custom design, proper SEO setup, accessibility, and someone who actually knows what they're doing.

This tier is where most St. Louis small businesses should be shopping. The key is knowing what to look for — more on that at the end of this article.

Agency — $5,000 to $50,000+

Agencies have overhead: account managers, project managers, designers, copywriters, developers. You're paying for that infrastructure whether you need it or not. For a small business that needs a 5 to 10 page website, that overhead rarely produces better results — it just costs more. Agencies make sense at a certain scale. For most St. Louis small businesses, they're a budget mismatch.

What Actually Drives the Price Up

Regardless of who builds your site, these factors push the price higher:

  • Custom design vs template. A site built from scratch costs more than one that starts from a template. But a well-customized template beats a poorly-executed custom build every time.
  • Number of pages. A 5-page site is simpler than a 20-page site. More pages means more content, more structure, more time.
  • Who writes the content. If you hand over finished text and photos, the project is faster and cheaper. If the designer has to source or write content, that's additional work.
  • E-commerce. Adding an online shop roughly doubles the complexity. Inventory management, payment processing, product pages, and shipping logic all add time and cost.
  • SEO setup. A site built properly for search from day one — with correct page titles, meta descriptions, schema markup, and fast load times — costs slightly more than one that ignores these things. It's worth it.

Hidden Costs Nobody Mentions Upfront

When comparing quotes, make sure you know what's included. These are often left out:

  • Domain name. About $15/year. Yours if you register it yourself — don't let the builder register it for you.
  • Hosting. Ranges from $0 (Netlify free tier) to $200+/year depending on the platform.
  • Ongoing maintenance. Who updates things when WordPress plugins conflict? Who fixes the contact form when it breaks? This should be a monthly retainer or a care plan — factor it into the real cost.
  • The rebuild in 3 to 5 years. Websites go stale. Design trends change, platforms get discontinued, your business evolves. A site is not a one-time purchase — it's an ongoing investment.

5 Questions to Ask Any Web Designer Before You Pay

  1. What exactly is included in this price? (content writing? hosting setup? SEO?)
  2. Do I own everything after it's built? (code, domain, hosting account — all of it)
  3. How do I make updates after launch? (and what does that cost?)
  4. Is SEO part of the build, or an add-on?
  5. What happens if something breaks six months from now?

A designer who gives you clear answers to all five is worth the price. One who deflects or gets defensive is telling you something.
