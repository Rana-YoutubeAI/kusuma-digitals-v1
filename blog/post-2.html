Why Isn't My Business
Showing on Google?

There's a reason — several, actually. Here's how to figure out which problem you have and what to do about it this week versus what takes more time.

By Rana  ·  June 1, 2026  ·  4 min read  ·  Back to Blog

This is one of the most frustrating things a small business owner can experience: you built a website, you registered your business, and when you search for yourself on Google — nothing. Or worse, a competitor shows up where you should be.

The first thing to know is that "showing up on Google" means three completely different things, and each one has a different cause and a different fix.

The Three Ways to Show Up on Google

1. Google Maps Pack (the 3 local listings with a map)

When someone searches "pizza near me" or "salon in South City," Google shows a map with three business listings above the regular search results. This is called the Maps Pack or Local Pack, and it's the most valuable real estate for a local business.

The Maps Pack is driven almost entirely by your Google Business Profile (GBP) — not your website.

2. Organic Search Results (the blue links)

These are the regular website listings below the map. Ranking here is driven by your website content, SEO setup, and how many other sites link to you. It takes longer than Maps but matters for searches that don't have obvious local intent.

3. Google Ads (labeled "Sponsored")

These are paid placements at the very top. You can appear here immediately — you just pay per click. We won't cover ads in this article. If you're asking why you're "not showing up," you almost certainly mean organic or Maps.

Why You're Not in the Maps Pack

In order, these are the most common reasons:

  • Your Google Business Profile isn't claimed. Go to business.google.com right now and search for your business. If it exists but is unclaimed, you'll see an option to claim it. If it doesn't exist, create it.
  • Your profile is incomplete. A GBP with no hours, no photos, a vague description, or the wrong category gets deprioritized. Google rewards completeness.
  • You have no reviews, or very few. Reviews are one of the strongest ranking signals for Maps. Three reviews isn't enough for a competitive search. Even 15 to 20 helps significantly.
  • Your service area isn't set correctly. If you're a service-area business (you go to the customer), make sure your GBP reflects that with a service area, not a hidden address.
  • Your listing is too new. GBP takes time to build authority. A brand-new listing won't rank for competitive terms immediately — give it 60 to 90 days of consistent activity.

Why Your Website Doesn't Rank in Organic

  • New sites take time. Google needs to discover, crawl, and assess your site. A brand-new website realistically takes 3 to 6 months to appear for most searches — not because anything is wrong, but because trust builds slowly.
  • Your pages don't include local keywords. If your page title says "Home" and your content never mentions St. Louis or your neighborhood, Google doesn't know where you serve.
  • Nobody links to your site. Links from other websites are one of Google's strongest ranking signals. A new site with zero inbound links has no authority yet.
  • Your competitors have history. A business that's been building its online presence for 3 years has a head start. You can catch up, but it's a marathon, not a sprint.

What to Do This Week — For Free

Start here. These cost nothing but your time:

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Fill out every single field. Business description, hours, service area, category (pick the most specific one that fits), and upload at least 5 photos.
  2. Ask your last 5 customers for a review. A personal message — text or email — gets responses. A generic "please leave a review" sign in your window gets ignored. Say: "Hey, would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It helps a lot." That's it.
  3. Check your NAP consistency. NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone. Your business name, address, and phone number must be identical everywhere: your website, your GBP, Yelp, Facebook, any local directories. Inconsistencies confuse Google and hurt your ranking.
  4. Add your city to your website's key pages. Your homepage title should include your city. Example: "Maria's Bakery — South City St. Louis" is better than just "Maria's Bakery."

What Takes Longer

Getting to the top of Maps for a competitive category in St. Louis — restaurants, salons, contractors — takes sustained effort over months. That means:

  • Regular posts and photo uploads to your GBP
  • A steady stream of new reviews over time (not 20 in one week, then nothing)
  • Building citations across 50+ local and industry directories
  • Creating content on your website that targets local keywords

This is what local SEO actually is — and it's the reason businesses that start early have such a significant advantage over late starters.
